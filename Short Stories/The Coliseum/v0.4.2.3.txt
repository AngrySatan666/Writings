[T] The Coliseum : 

[P1] The Coliseum and Treasure :

	{SS1} Reflecting Attachments to the Coliseum : 
	{Every night, I return to this crumbling ruin, driven by a question I can never quite answer. The night air presses heavy with the smell of damp stone and earth. Wind tugs at my cloak, biting cold and firm against my skin, like the weight of unanswered questions pressing deep into my bones. I walk the same path I’ve walked countless nights. As a kid I used to always imagine this place full of Kings and Queens from nations I haven’t even heard of before. Countless nights I dreamed of standing in the coliseum’s heart—a warrior, a champion. But I seem to be the only one who remembers this massive place. But I've been here so long that the act of searching has become more than just a habit. It feels more like one of that crack-pot wizard’s rituals now. This endless search for something I can’t name—a buried treasure only I seem to believe these ruins guard. Each step feels heavier than the last, every sound is amplified in the stillness: the crunch of gravel under my boots, the whisper of wind threading through cracks in the walls, and the faint shifting of dust, soft and relentless, clinging to me like it wants to pull me back into the earth.} 

	{SS2} The Coliseum is "Alive" : 
	{The ruins rise ahead of me, jagged and towering, silhouetted against the hollow glow of the moon. There’s a presence here, faint but undeniable. The coliseum looms like something waiting, its shattered walls yawning wide like broken jaws; pillars standing tall as if they bear witness to my every move. I stalk the perimeter like a hunter circling prey, though what I’m hunting, I still don’t know. For a moment, as the moonlight flickers against the stones, I think I hear it—the distant roar of a crowd, faint and fleeting, as if the ruins are breathing out the ghosts of their glory. The sound fades, leaving only silence, and yet it lingers in my chest, a phantom weight pressing down.}
	
	{SS3} My Endless Search for? : 
	{Is it treasure I’m searching for? Or just proof that something worth finding ever existed here?
Maybe it’s something I’ve convinced myself is hidden here—something precious, something that will finally make all of this make sense. Or maybe it’s just the routine that keeps me coming back. The act of searching, even when I no longer know what I’m searching for. I keep telling myself it’s a hidden spark of meaning I seek, but the truth is, I’ve walked this path so many times now that I’ve forgotten what I’m even looking for. I only know I can’t stop.}



[P2] Myself In The Dust :

	{SS1} Acknowledging the Dust as once Significant : 
	{The dust shifts with every step, coating my hands as I steady myself against the crumbling walls. Each grain of dust whirls in the wind, as if clawing its way free from this place. The dust—it’s all that remains of something greater, fragments of dreams and influence, reduced to nothing more than powder at my tattered feet. Even the pebbles beneath my feet are the debris of what once held up the sky. These shards and broken fragments, every last grain, once belonged to something whole: were once ceilings that crowned the coliseum, the mortar that held the walls, the statues that inspired awe, soaring high above the throngs of people who gathered here.}

	{SS2} This Dust is now just Dust :
	{Now, they’ve fallen—just rubble underfoot, discarded, forgotten. Generations labored to raise it from the dust, and now, in the blink of a lifetime, it crumbles. I can’t help but wonder: was it inevitable? Or was its destruction our own doing? Now it’s scattered and weightless, too insubstantial to rebuild with and yet too heavy to ignore. How does something so magnificent, so unshakable, fall to ruin? I wonder how much of myself is in this dust, how much of what I once was has been ground into nothing but debris. Were these battles for glory? For survival? Or for nothing at all? I wonder, were these walls ever strong enough to hold such violence—and was I?
}



[P3] Peering Through The Window :

	{SS1} An Odd Window to the Heart :
	{I glance toward a window—an odd, lonely wound carved into this place of stone. The moonlight brushes against the jagged edges of the opening, casting uneven shadows inside the crumbling stone. Why is it here, in a building of walls and bricks meant to keep the world out? It feels like it doesn’t belong, an afterthought in a place that was once so sure of its purpose. I think it was meant to be beautiful once—a bay window where the light could pour in, illuminating everything within. A way straight to the coliseum's heart. But no light reaches inside anymore. The glass is gone, the frame warped, and deep cracks spread across their surfaces like scars carved by unseen hands. Outside the window lies a barren expanse, where weeds struggle to grow and the air feels heavy, unmoving. It offers no reprieve, only another reminder of what’s been lost. This window wasn’t always here. Someone made it, a deep wound chipped into the stone with deliberate care, and we all stood by and let them. Looking at it now, I feel a pang of regret, as if I had been complicit in its creation, letting them carve away what was once whole. Was it meant to be a beacon, or just a scar I could never cover?}


[P4] The Museum's Disaster :
	
	{SS1} Introducing the Museum :
	{It wasn’t just one group though. There were many—a parade of self-proclaimed restorers, with grand promises to restore this place. They arrived with banners and tools, eager to sweep away the rubble and raise the coliseum from its knees. I remember their hands—so many hands—chiseling away, their tools sharp and unrelenting. They promised it would bring something beautiful to the darkened halls. The people believed them, perhaps because they needed to. I’m not entirely sure why I believed them. Perhaps it just felt easier to trust their hammers than face the cracks on my own. But they misunderstood it. They stripped away the fragments that gave it meaning, leaving it emptier than before. When their gold ran dry, they vanished, abandoning their half-built illusions and leaving behind a ruin that no longer recognized itself.}
	
	{SS2} The Workers and Their Promises :
	{Their tools clanging against the stone, their voices full of the promises we all wanted to believe. One said they could smooth the cracks in the walls, but their work now lay before me splintered and raw, fragile to the touch. Another came for the columns, swearing they would stand taller when they were done. But their hammers seem to have only deepened the fractures, and the pillars grew more unstable. And then there was the one who worked at the center—the foundation itself. They dug deep, claiming they could reinforce what was broken. They said they could make this monument whole again. But now that they’ve left, nothing but a gaping hole where the heart of this place used to reside. I can almost swear that each one left this once beautiful place worse and worse. Until finally, we stopped hoping for restoration at all, and they too, decided it was a lost cause. We stood silent as their hammers cracked the stone, not realizing what we were giving up. But now this window stands not as a gift, but as a scar.}



[P5] What it Used to Be :

	{SS1} The Coliseum was Beautiful for All :
	{I can’t help but sympathize with the coliseum. Thousands of pounds of gold and silver, thousands of miles crossed for materials—everything poured into its creation. The greatest architects of their time bled and toiled to raise something that would stand against the march of time. And for a while, it worked. This was a place where fears were laid to rest, sorrows eased, and hungry mouths fed.  Back then, this place thrummed with life—laughter bouncing off the walls, the warm smell of bread mingling with the clang of swords. The coliseum was a sanctuary for all where these very walls erased division. Noblemen laughed with peasants; the rich broke bread with the starving. Here, for fleeting moments, everyone was simply human. We gathered not as strangers but as equals, breathing the same air, sharing the same dreams. I remember hearing the stories of those that once stood among them, feeling the pulse of belonging. But in the blink of an eye, it was reduced to rubble and ash.}	

	{SS2} Going Deeper in the Window :
	{As I step deeper into the ruins, I can almost hear the clash of swords echoing through the hollow halls. The walls still bear the stains of blood spilled in honor of distant kings, their faces long forgotten. My breath catches as the ghosts of its prime rise around me. I can hear the roaring crowd, their cheers shaking the air. Fighters move through their stances, blades gleaming under the sun’s gaze. Blacksmiths work tirelessly, sparks flying as they prepare tools for victory—or for defeat. And somewhere, a king speaks, not as a tyrant but as an equal, his words uniting rather than dividing. But then the ghosts fade, leaving only silence. The crowd is gone. The fighters have disappeared. Only the broken stones, and dried blood from the fallen remain.}
	
	{SS3} The Columns :
	{As I near the center of the coliseum, the columns rise like ancient sentinels, their cracked surfaces towering in the moonlight. They tell a story, each one shaped by the hands of a different artist, their meanings carved into the stone as though the truth of this place could only be borne in fragments. No two are the same. One bears jagged spirals of grief, winding upward as if to strangle the heavens. Another is etched with the delicate latticework of love turned to ash, its fragile patterns worn smooth by time. Some are marked with scenes of triumph, warriors frozen in stone mid-victory, their faces chipped away by the years. Others bear softer designs: the outstretched hands of lovers caught in their final embrace, or the branches of a tree winding up the column, its leaves now eroded into faint shadows. Each carving feels like a confession, immortalized in stone and then slowly forgotten. But one column is different—smooth and unadorned, its surface untouched by the artist’s hand. It draws my attention, stark against the cacophony of carvings around it. I pause in front of it, wondering why it was left blank. Perhaps its creator had too much to say and could never settle on the right design. Or maybe it was meant to hold something fragile, something too precious to carve in stone. Whatever the reason, it stands out among its brethren, as lonely and silent as the ruins around it.}

[P6] Left with Nothing but the Rubble :

	{SS1} Here Alone :
	{Now the columns stand for no one and for no reason. Their origin is forgotten, their purpose erased. But I suppose that’s not entirely true, is it? I’m still here, standing in the center of it all, searching for the treasure that will redeem this place, trying to make sense of the rubble. There’s no one left to share the silence, no crowd to cheer, no king to honor. I place my hand against one of the columns, feeling its surface cold and crumbling beneath my touch. As I trace the jagged cracks winding upward, I wonder if these fractures run as deep as the hearts that built them? No probably not, for its just me and these hollowed stones, wondering if there’s anything left worth rebuilding—or if it’s time to let the dust take it all. As I look up into the stars of the Milky Way, a single tear begins to form. But even with all the pain, time, national efforts, countless sleepless nights, money, and bloodshed all taken for granted, this tear refuses to fall. Is the fate of this place so drastic that it’s numbed even me to all emotion? I can no longer tell. Because just as these columns were once considered one of the world’s biggest riches, it now lies worth no more than a bucket of dirt. The same way I keep searching for something I may never find. Like these columns, I reach for the sky, never knowing why.}
	
	{SS2} The Plaque :
	{To see such a massive and once vital place to the world, now completely forgotten to the masses, only surpassed by faint memories and the scurrying of hungry mice, is nothing short of the saddest tale in mankind’s history. A history this grandiose place is no longer a part of. As my only tear manages to stream, I look up at the blank column to wipe the soot-filled tear from my cheek and notice a small plaque at the top. Faded by years of sun and dust, it holds the only text still readable here. I glance back at the window. Its jagged edges seem to whisper a quiet truth, the light outside failing to penetrate. Even the window seemed to know it, its jagged edges whispering a bitter truth I've always known but avoided all along. Promises break and hope demands too high a price, the very same broken promises etched into this plaque.}

	{SS3} Not all promises :
	{"Not all promises are worth the Price of Hope". And yet, I stand here, still searching for something more.}
