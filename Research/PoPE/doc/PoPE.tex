\section{Introduction}
    \subsection{Where We Stand}
        \hspace{1em}
        Our current understanding of reality is hinged upon two pillars of scientific study.
        \emph{Quantum Mechanics}, the rules of the very small.
        and \emph{Relativity}, the rules of the very large.

        These two pillars, while individually robust and well-tested, remain fundamentally disconnected.
        Where quantum mechanics governs the \emph{dynamically} probabilistic behaviors of particles and fields and their propagation throughout space and time.
        But also their interactions with quantum fields, other particles, their own wavefunctions, and their environment.
        And where relativity describes the \emph{deterministic} geometric structures of spacetime itself, and the laws of physics that guide matter and energy within.

        Standard quantum mechanics treats superposition as ontic and collapse as a probabilistic projection often tied to observation.
        Relativity treats space and time as geometric backgrounds shaped by energy.
        These frameworks remain disconnected and struggle with dark matter, dark energy, and the origin of time.

        We unify these domains by reinterpreting collapse as the primary physical process.
        Energy always collapses; persistence emerges only via \emph{anchoring}.
        Space, time, and matter arise statistically from successful persistence.
        This \emph{collapse--churn} picture reframes quantum indeterminacy and relativistic geometry as consequences of the same substrate dynamics.
